# Domain Adaptation and Sim-to-Real Generalization for Gravitational Lens Classifiers

Scope note: this thread covers domain adaptation, domain randomization, GAN-based image translation, and self-supervised pretraining as sim-to-real techniques. Fine-tuning depth and lens-injection strategies are explicitly out of scope (covered by a separate thread, `finetuning_and_lens_injection.md`), and are only referenced here where a source bundles them with the in-scope techniques for contrast.

## Has formal domain adaptation (DANN/Ganin & Lempitsky 2015; CORAL; MMD; adversarial DA) been applied to astronomical classification, specifically lens finding or closely related sim-to-real tasks, and with what measured improvement?

### Takeaway
Yes — this is the most mature and best-quantified of the four technique areas. A cluster of papers (primarily from Ćiprijanović et al. at Fermilab/collaborators, and Gleyzer/Toomey/Alabama collaborators) has applied DANN, MMD, CORAL-adjacent, ADDA, WDGRL, AdaMatch, and optimal-transport feature alignment directly to gravitational-lens-finding, lens-parameter-regression, dark-matter-substructure classification, galaxy-morphology classification, and galaxy-merger classification, in every case training on one domain (simulated or less-realistic data) and evaluating on a harder/real target domain. Reported gains range from several AUROC points to accuracy jumps of 20-40+ percentage points, consistently showing domain adaptation substantially -- though not completely -- closes the sim-to-real gap relative to naive training.

### Cited Findings
- Ganin & Lempitsky's domain-adversarial training of neural networks (DANN) was originally published as "Unsupervised Domain Adaptation by Backpropagation" (ICML 2015, arXiv:1409.7495) and later as "Domain-Adversarial Training of Neural Networks" (JMLR 2016) -- foundational method, not astronomy-specific. -- [arXiv:1409.7495](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.7495)
- Swierc, Zhao, Ćiprijanović & Nord, "Domain Adaptation for Measurements of Strong Gravitational Lenses" (NeurIPS 2023 ML4PS workshop, arXiv:2311.17238): apply DANN and Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) to Einstein-radius (theta_E) regression, training on a "basic" simulated-lens source domain and evaluating on a harder target domain simulated with Dark Energy Survey (DES) noise characteristics; described as "the first application of domain adaptation for a regression task in strong lensing imaging analysis." Both DANN and MMD "significantly improve the model performance on the more complex target domain dataset" (abstract-level claim; specific numeric before/after table not retrievable from the abstract/HTML page fetched). -- [arXiv:2311.17238](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17238)
- Parul, Gleyzer, Reddy & Toomey, "Domain adaptation in application to gravitational lens finding" (arXiv:2410.01203, Oct 2024): compare ADDA (Adversarial Discriminative DA), WDGRL (Wasserstein Distance Guided Representation Learning), and SDA (Supervised DA via contrastive semantic alignment). Source domain = 30,000 simulated lenses + 30,000 non-lensed galaxies built with Lenstronomy on real galaxy images; target/test = real Hyper Suprime-Cam (HSC) Subaru Strategic Program data (1,754 real lens candidates + 1,754 non-lenses for adaptation; 200 real lenses + 20,000 non-lenses for the imbalanced real-world test). Unsupervised DA with an equivariant NN (ENN) encoder: naive baseline AUROC 0.921 -> ADDA AUROC 0.942, WDGRL AUROC 0.940; true-positive rate at 1% false-positive rate rose from 0.354 (baseline) to 0.528 with WDGRL (~49% relative gain). Supervised DA (SDA) on top of a real-data-trained baseline moved AUROC from 0.987 to 0.991; with spiral galaxies included in the target set, SDA reached AUROC 0.993 and TPR at 1% FPR of 0.897, specifically targeting spirals as a common false-positive class. -- [arXiv:2410.01203](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.01203v1)
- Ćiprijanović, Lewis, Pedro, Madireddy, Nord, Perdue & Wild, "DeepAstroUDA: Semi-Supervised Universal Domain Adaptation for Cross-Survey Galaxy Morphology Classification and Anomaly Detection" (NeurIPS 2022 ML4PS workshop, arXiv:2211.00677; extended version arXiv:2302.02005): a Universal DA method for cross-survey adaptation (SDSS<->DECaLS; LSST-mock 1-year vs 10-year depth; SDSS wide vs Stripe 82 deep field), applied to a 3-class morphology+anomaly task and a 10-class granular morphology task that includes gravitational lens detection as one of the (anomalous) classes. For the "High-to-Low" depth-adaptation scenario, target-domain F1 rises from 0.32 +/- 0.31 (no adaptation) to 0.73 +/- 0.13 (with DeepAstroUDA) -- described elsewhere as "up to 40%" accuracy improvement on unlabeled target data. -- [arXiv:2211.00677 / arXiv:2302.02005](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.00677)
- Ćiprijanović et al., "DeepAdversaries: Examining the Robustness of Deep Learning Models for Galaxy Morphology Classification" (arXiv:2112.14299): uses MMD-based domain adaptation (DANN/CORAL named as alternatives but not implemented) to adapt a classifier trained on low-noise LSST-like "Y10" (10-year depth) simulated galaxy images to noisier "Y1" (1-year depth) images, and tests robustness to one-pixel adversarial attacks. For ResNet18 on the noisy Y1 target domain: regular (non-adapted) training reaches 43% accuracy vs 66% accuracy with MMD domain adaptation (23-percentage-point gain). For a plain ConvNet: 48% -> 57% (9-point gain). -- [arXiv:2112.14299](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.14299)
- Ćiprijanović et al., "DeepMerge II: Building Robust Deep Learning Algorithms for Merging Galaxy Identification Across Domains" (arXiv:2103.01373, 2021): applies MMD, DANN, Fisher loss, and entropy minimization to merging-galaxy classification. Sim-to-sim case (Illustris-1 pristine vs. noise-added images at z=2): accuracy without adaptation ~58-60% -> ~77-79% with MMD/adversarial training (up to ~21-point gain). Sim-to-real case (Illustris-1 simulated galaxies at z=0 vs. real SDSS telescope observations): accuracy without adaptation 50% -> 69% with MMD + transfer learning (~19-point gain) -- a direct, quantified sim-to-real classification improvement on a task structurally analogous to lens finding (binary morphology classification, simulation source domain, real-survey target domain). -- [arXiv:2103.01373](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2103.01373)
- Alexander, Gleyzer, Reddy, Tidball & Toomey, "Domain Adaptation for Simulation-Based Dark Matter Searches Using Strong Gravitational Lensing" (arXiv:2112.12121): tests ADDA, AdaMatch, and Self-Ensemble UDA, plus D2-equivariant neural networks (ENNs), for classifying dark matter substructure signatures (none / CDM subhalo / superfluid DM vortex) in simulated strong-lens images, adapting from a fixed-redshift/fixed-SNR source domain (z=0.5, SNR~20) to a more variable target domain (z=0.4-0.6, SNR=10-30). ResNet-18+ADDA: AUC 0.880->0.955 (~59%->86% accuracy); ENN+ADDA: AUC 0.856->0.980 (~68%->91% accuracy); ENN+AdaMatch reaches AUC~0.960 (~86% accuracy). -- [arXiv:2112.12121](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.12121)
- "From Simulations to Surveys: Domain Adaptation for Galaxy Observations" (NeurIPS 2025 workshop, arXiv:2511.18590): trains on simulated TNG50 galaxies (source) and evaluates on real SDSS galaxies (target) for 3-class morphology classification (elliptical/spiral/irregular), using feature-level optimal-transport-based domain losses (entropic Sinkhorn OT, energy distance, Gaussian MMD, via the GeomLoss library) across CNN, E(2)-steerable CNN, and ResNet-18 backbones. With the best alignment configuration (Euclidean distance, scheduled alignment weights, top-k matching), target macro-F1 rises from ~46% (no adaptation; ~30% in a stricter metric) to ~87% (~62.6% in that stricter metric), with the domain classifier's AUC driven down to ~0.5 (i.e., the adapted features are statistically indistinguishable between sim and real domains). This is one of the most direct and largest quantified sim-to-real gains found in this search. -- [arXiv:2511.18590](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.18590v1)

### Inferences
- The consistent, large (double-digit percentage point) gains across these studies -- spanning lens finding proper (Parul et al.), lens-parameter regression (Swierc et al.), dark-matter-substructure classification on lens images (Alexander et al.), and the closely analogous tasks of galaxy morphology/merger classification (Ćiprijanović et al., TNG50->SDSS paper) -- strongly suggest that applying one or more of these DA techniques (DANN, MMD, ADDA, WDGRL, optimal-transport alignment) directly to a Zoobot-style lens classifier's feature space would likely close a meaningful fraction of a sim-to-real gap, particularly because several of these papers use the same tooling (Lenstronomy sims, ResNet/equivariant backbones) typical of lens-finding pipelines. [Inference, not a citation.]
- Unsupervised DA (no real labels needed in the target domain) consistently underperforms supervised DA / direct fine-tuning on real labels where both are tested (e.g., Parul et al.: WDGRL AUROC 0.940 unsupervised vs. 0.987 real-data-supervised baseline, improved only marginally to 0.991 by SDA) -- suggesting DA is best framed as a way to make the most of whatever small amount of real/labeled target data exists, not a full substitute for some real labels. [Inference, not a citation.]

### Gaps
- None of the fetched abstracts/pages for Swierc et al. (arXiv:2311.17238) gave a specific numeric before/after table (only qualitative "significantly improve" language) -- the full paper likely has it but was not extracted in this pass.
- No study found applies DANN/CORAL/MMD-style domain adaptation to a Zoobot-architecture classifier specifically (all examples use ResNet, plain CNN, or equivariant NN backbones); whether gains transfer to a GZ-pretrained Zoobot backbone is untested in the literature surveyed.
- Could not confirm the final peer-reviewed publication venue (vs. workshop-only status) for several of these papers (e.g., DeepAdversaries, DeepMerge II, DeepAstroUDA) within the tool budget available.

## What does the domain randomization literature (Tobin et al. 2017, arXiv:1703.06907) say, and has it been applied in astronomical ML / lens-finding / galaxy morphology specifically?

### Takeaway
Domain randomization is a well-established robotics sim-to-real technique (randomize simulator textures, lighting, physics so the model cannot overfit to any one simulator's artifacts) with no astronomy-specific paper found that explicitly adopts the "domain randomization" framing or cites Tobin et al. directly for a lens-finding or galaxy-morphology application; however, astronomical lens-finding pipelines already randomize observational nuisance parameters (noise, PSF, background) as standard simulation practice, which is functionally similar in spirit though not framed or validated as a deliberate generalization strategy the way Tobin et al. frame it.

### Cited Findings
- Tobin, Fong, Ray, Schneider, Zaremba & Abbeel, "Domain Randomization for Transferring Deep Neural Networks from Simulation to the Real World" (arXiv:1703.06907, 2017): randomizes rendering parameters (textures, lighting, camera position, object/physics properties) in simulation so that "the real world may just look like another variation" to the network; demonstrated transferring an object detector trained purely on simulated images with non-realistic random textures to real-world robotic grasping with ~1.5 cm accuracy, representing (per the paper) the first successful transfer of a deep network trained only on simulated RGB images to real-world robotic control without any real images in training. -- [arXiv:1703.06907](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1703.06907)
- General domain-randomization literature describes the technique as training "to maximize performance over a diverse set of simulation models... with parameters sampled randomly from a given task distribution," explicitly to force domain-invariant features rather than overfitting to one simulator's idiosyncrasies -- the same underlying goal posed in this research question. -- [Domain randomization glossary](https://voxel51.com/glossary/domain-randomization)
- Follow-on robotics work (e.g., Bayesian Domain Randomization, "Adversarial Domain Randomization," arXiv:1812.00491; domain randomization for in-orbit 6D pose estimation, arXiv:2406.11743) extends the technique with adaptive/learned randomization distributions, but these are non-astronomy or orbital-engineering (spacecraft pose, not sky-survey imaging) applications. -- [arXiv:2406.11743](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.11743)
- No search returned an astronomical ML paper that cites Tobin et al. (2017) directly or frames its noise/PSF/background-randomization training strategy explicitly as "domain randomization" in the robotics sense, for either lens finding or galaxy morphology classification.

### Inferences
- Standard practice in simulation-based lens-finding training pipelines already randomizes nuisance simulation parameters -- noise realizations, PSF width, backgrounds, source light profiles -- across training examples (this is visible, for instance, in Lenstronomy-based lens-injection pipelines used across several of the domain-adaptation papers above, e.g., Parul et al. 2410.01203's use of Lenstronomy-simulated lenses over real galaxy backgrounds). This is conceptually close to domain randomization, but the astronomy literature treats it as ordinary data augmentation for realism rather than as a deliberate strategy explicitly validated (via ablation against a fixed, non-randomized simulator) to produce domain-invariant features in the Tobin et al. sense. Applying the discipline more rigorously -- e.g., deliberately over-randomizing noise/PSF/background ranges beyond the best estimate of the real distribution, as Tobin et al. do with "non-realistic random textures" -- is plausibly a complementary, low-cost technique for a sim-trained lens classifier, worth testing as an ablation (train with narrow realistic ranges vs. deliberately widened ranges) since it requires no real labeled data and no architecture changes. [Inference, not a citation.]
- Because no quantitative astronomy example of deliberate domain randomization (as opposed to domain adaptation) was found, any expected benefit for a lens classifier is an extrapolation from the robotics literature and from the implicit noise/PSF randomization already present in sim-to-real lens-finding comparisons (e.g., the Euclid Q1 "simulations to sky" result below), not a direct empirical result. [Inference, not a citation.]

### Gaps
- No astronomy-specific "domain randomization" paper (explicitly named/framed as such) for lens finding or galaxy morphology classification was found; this is a genuine literature gap, not a search failure -- multiple targeted queries (varying phrasing, searching for the Tobin citation plus astronomy keywords) returned only the general robotics literature and the (separately covered) domain-adaptation papers.
- Could not locate a controlled ablation in any lens-finding paper that isolates the effect of randomization range/diversity of simulation nuisance parameters (as opposed to simply using "realistic" fixed distributions) on real-data generalization.

## Has GAN-based image-to-image translation (CycleGAN, Zhu et al. 2017, arXiv:1703.10593) been used to translate simulated astronomical images toward real survey appearance before classifier training, and with what measured improvement?

### Takeaway
There is no clean CycleGAN-for-lens-classifier-training example in the literature surveyed, but there are several adjacent, confirmed astronomy applications of GAN-based image-to-image translation for closing simulation/real appearance gaps (PSF/deconvolution learning via CycleGAN, GAN-based denoising of lensing maps, and a lens-finding paper that explicitly combines simulation, augmentation, semi-supervised learning, and GANs to reduce simulation artifacts, reporting an order-of-magnitude precision/recall improvement over naive sim-only training).

### Cited Findings
- Zhu, Park, Isola & Efros, "Unpaired Image-to-Image Translation using Cycle-Consistent Adversarial Networks" (ICCV 2017, arXiv:1703.10593) -- foundational CycleGAN method, not astronomy-specific. -- [arXiv:1703.10593](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.10593)
- A CycleGAN-based method restores astronomical images and jointly estimates the point-spread function (PSF) and a deconvolution kernel by training on simulated high-resolution astronomical images paired (in an unpaired/cycle-consistent sense) with real observed blurred images; the CycleGAN uses two generators -- a "PSF-Gen" learning the PSF and a "Dec-Gen" learning the deconvolution kernel -- and was tested on real solar-telescope and small-aperture-telescope data. This is a direct astronomy example of using CycleGAN-style unpaired translation to bridge a simulation/real appearance gap, though applied to image restoration/PSF estimation rather than to pre-processing training data for a classifier. -- [Astronomical image restoration and PSF estimation with deep learning, Proc. SPIE 11203, 112030Q](https://photonicsforenergy.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/11203/112030Q/Astronomical-image-restoration-and-point-spread-function-estimation-with-deep/10.1117/12.2541083.full)
- Sheng, Vasan G.C., Choi, Sharpnack & Jones, "An Unsupervised Hunt for Gravitational Lenses" (AISTATS 2022, arXiv:2210.11681): directly targets the sim-to-real gap in lens finding by combining "simulation, data augmentation, semi-supervised learning, and GANs," using adversarial networks specifically to reduce the simulation artifacts that a naive classifier would otherwise learn as spurious lens-identifying features. The combined approach is reported to achieve a performance improvement of "an order of magnitude" over naive ResNet training on simulated data alone, while maintaining both high precision and high recall -- a directly relevant, quantified (if only approximately so) example of GAN-assisted sim-to-real closing for lens finding specifically. -- [arXiv:2210.11681](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.11681)
- A conditional-GAN image-to-image translation approach was used for noise reduction in weak-lensing mass maps from Subaru Hyper Suprime-Cam (HSC) first-year data, training on 30,000 sets of mock HSC catalogues that directly incorporate observational effects, to translate from noisy maps to denoised maps while preserving non-Gaussian cosmological information -- a GAN-based translation targeting realistic-observation fidelity, though for mass-mapping/denoising rather than lens classification per se. -- [arXiv:1911.12890](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.12890)
- "Model-Assisted GAN" (MAGAN) approaches have been used to generate large datasets of gravitational lensing simulations efficiently by conditioning GAN generation on target physical parameters, primarily to speed up/augment simulation volume rather than to translate the appearance of existing sims toward real-survey statistics. -- [Gravitational Lensing with Generative Adversarial Networks](https://digitalcommons.imsa.edu/sir_presentations/2020/session2/73)

### Inferences
- No source found trains a classifier directly on CycleGAN-translated (sim->real-appearance) images and reports a clean before/after accuracy/completeness table in the way the domain-adaptation papers above do; the closest analogue (Sheng et al. 2022, AISTATS, arXiv:2210.11681) folds GAN-based artifact suppression into a broader pipeline (simulation + augmentation + semi-supervised learning + GANs) and reports only an aggregate "order of magnitude" gain, not an ablated GAN-only contribution. A dedicated CycleGAN pre-processing step (translate Lenstronomy sims toward real HSC/DES/DESI noise and PSF statistics, then train the classifier normally) therefore remains, as far as this search could determine, untested in isolation in the lens-finding literature -- a promising and apparently open experimental gap given the technique's demonstrated success in the closely related PSF/deconvolution domain. [Inference, not a citation.]
- Given that CycleGAN-style translation has already been shown in astronomy to learn PSF and noise characteristics from unpaired real/simulated image sets (the PSF-Gen/Dec-Gen paper above), it is plausible this could be repurposed as a sim->real pre-processing step for a lens classifier (translate simulated cutouts to have DES/DESI/HSC-like noise and PSF before training), but this specific application has not been demonstrated or quantified in the sources found. [Inference, not a citation.]

### Gaps
- Could not find the authorship/year detail for the CycleGAN PSF/deconvolution paper beyond the SPIE conference proceedings page (Proc. SPIE 11203, 112030Q) -- the full bibliographic citation (first author, year) was not resolved within the tool budget.
- Could not verify a quantitative ablation isolating the GAN component's contribution in Sheng et al. 2022 (arXiv:2210.11681) beyond the aggregate "order of magnitude" claim -- the full paper likely contains this breakdown but was not extracted.
- A separate lead, titled roughly "Exploring domain adaptation with generative..." (fragment from kspa.soe.ucsc.edu/sites/default/files/Ribli.pdf, surfaced via search but PDF content was not extractable in this session), may be directly relevant (possibly Ribli et al., generative-model-based domain adaptation for lensing) but could not be verified or cited with confidence -- flagged here as an unconfirmed lead for the report writer or a follow-up search, not a citation.

## What does the self-supervised pretraining literature (contrastive learning, MAEs) on unlabeled real survey images say, and is any of it applied to lens finding specifically?

### Takeaway
Self-supervised pretraining on large unlabeled real-survey datasets is a well-developed and quantitatively validated astronomy technique (Hayat et al. 2021, Stein et al. 2021, Walmsley et al.'s Zoobot/hybrid work, AstroCLIP), consistently showing that pretraining on unlabeled real images reduces the labeled-data requirement by roughly 2-4x and improves downstream task accuracy; one dedicated lens-finding paper (LenSiam, arXiv:2311.10100) directly demonstrates that a lensing-specific self-supervised pretraining scheme beats both generic self-supervised and generic supervised (ImageNet) pretraining on an Einstein-radius regression task, and Stein et al.'s self-supervised embeddings of tens of millions of real DESI Legacy Survey images have been used to search for strong lens candidates via similarity search, though without a quantified completeness/efficiency number in the sources retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- Hayat, Harrington, Stein, Lukić & Mustafa, "Self-Supervised Representation Learning for Astronomical Images" (arXiv:2012.13083, Dec 2020) and the companion "Estimating Galactic Distances From Images Using Self-Supervised Representation Learning" (arXiv:2101.04293, Jan 2021): train a contrastive self-supervised framework (SimCLR-style) on SDSS multi-band galaxy photometry with astronomy-specific augmentations (rotation, jittering, additive Gaussian noise, synthetic galactic-dust extinction) in addition to standard computer-vision augmentations. Pretraining on a large unlabeled corpus followed by fine-tuning on limited labels attains the accuracy of a fully-supervised model that requires 2-4x more labeled data; fine-tuning the self-supervised representations using all available SDSS Main Galaxy Sample labels outperforms the prior state-of-the-art fully-supervised method on redshift estimation. -- [arXiv:2012.13083](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2012.13083); [arXiv:2101.04293](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2101.04293)
- Stein, Harrington, Blaum, Medan & Lukić, "Self-supervised similarity search for large scientific datasets" (NeurIPS 2021 ML4PS workshop, arXiv:2110.13151): train a self-supervised model on tens of millions of galaxy images (reported as 42 million in the paper; summarized elsewhere as up to 76 million) from the DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys Data Release 9 to produce representations "robust to symmetries, uncertainties, and noise," enabling semantic similarity search; the paper explicitly demonstrates using these representations and similarity-search/simple-linear-classifier tools to identify strong gravitational lens candidates, and states the approach also "improves supervised training datasets" and "accelerates crowdsourcing campaigns" -- but no specific completeness/purity/efficiency number for the lens-finding application was retrievable from the abstract-level content fetched. -- [arXiv:2110.13151](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.13151)
- Walmsley et al., work combining contrastive self-supervised learning (BYOL-based) with supervised pretraining on Galaxy Zoo volunteer labels ("hybrid" pretraining), using GZ-Evo v0.1 (96.5 million volunteer responses across 552,000 labeled galaxy images plus 1.34 million unlabeled galaxies): the hybrid approach -- jointly combining contrastive learning and supervised pretraining -- improves downstream classification accuracy even when abundant downstream labels are already available, and is noted as well-suited to Euclid and Rubin because it leverages both a decade of human labels and the much larger volume of unlabeled imaging these surveys will produce; this hybrid approach was reportedly deployed (as of ~August 2024) to measure galaxy morphology across Euclid data. -- [Towards Galaxy Foundation Models with Hybrid Contrastive Learning, arXiv:2206.11927](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2206.11927)
- Zoobot (Walmsley et al.) is a released Python package providing deep-learning models pretrained (via supervised representation learning on Galaxy Zoo DECaLS labels) for fine-tuning to new galaxy-image tasks without requiring deep-learning expertise; models trained to answer every Galaxy Zoo DECaLS question are shown to learn meaningful semantic representations transferable to tasks the model was never directly trained on. -- [Practical galaxy morphology tools from deep supervised representation learning](https://oro.open.ac.uk/82334)
- Lanusse et al., "AstroCLIP: Cross-Modal Pre-Training for Astronomical Foundation Models" (NeurIPS 2023 ML4PS workshop, arXiv:2310.03024): cross-modal contrastive pretraining aligning separately self-supervised-pretrained image and spectrum transformer encoders on DESI multi-band images and spectra; resulting embeddings enable competitive zero-/few-shot prediction (without further fine-tuning) of redshift, stellar mass, and morphology classification, and support accurate cross-modal and in-modality semantic similarity search. No lens-finding-specific application or number is reported in the retrieved content. -- [arXiv:2310.03024](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2310.03024)
- Chang, Huang, Fagin, Chan & Lin, "LenSiam: Self-Supervised Learning on Strong Gravitational Lens Images" (arXiv:2311.10100, 2023): a SimSiam-based self-supervised framework with a lensing-specific image augmentation scheme, pretrained on 100,000 paired (200,000 total) simulated strong-lens images built from real COSMOS 23.5/25.2 galaxy images plus analytic source models via Lenstronomy, with lensing parameters never exposed during self-supervised pretraining. Fine-tuned for Einstein-radius regression on the "Lens Challenge" dataset (1,000 train / 1,000 test Euclid-like images): LenSiam-pretrained R^2 = 0.586, vs. generic SimSiam (default, non-lens-specific augmentations) R^2 = 0.426, vs. supervised ImageNet-1k pretraining R^2 = 0.360 -- i.e., lensing-domain-specific self-supervised pretraining beats both a generic self-supervised baseline and a generic supervised-pretraining baseline by a wide margin (~37.6% relative improvement in R^2 over the ImageNet-supervised baseline) on a lens-measurement task. This is a direct, quantified, lens-specific self-supervised pretraining result, though pretrained on simulated (not real) lens images rather than unlabeled real survey data. -- [arXiv:2311.10100](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.10100)

### Inferences
- The LenSiam result (pretraining strategy matters more than generic ImageNet transfer, and domain-specific augmentations beat generic computer-vision augmentations) combined with the Hayat et al./Stein et al. results (large real, unlabeled survey corpora materially improve downstream label efficiency) together suggest that pretraining Zoobot (or an equivalent backbone) with a contrastive/self-supervised objective on large volumes of unlabeled real survey cutouts -- rather than (or in addition to) its existing Galaxy-Zoo-label-supervised pretraining -- could plausibly improve real-world lens-finding generalization, especially if combined with lensing-specific augmentations analogous to LenSiam's. This specific combination (self-supervised pretrain on real unlabeled survey images, then fine-tune for lens finding specifically) has not, however, been directly demonstrated in any source found -- LenSiam pretrains on simulated lens images, and Stein/Hayat et al. pretrain on real images but for redshift/morphology/similarity-search, not lens classification fine-tuning. [Inference, not a citation.]
- Walmsley's hybrid self-supervised + supervised pretraining direction (GZ-Evo, BYOL-based), explicitly motivated by the scale mismatch between abundant unlabeled Euclid/Rubin imaging and limited human labels, is architecturally the closest existing precedent to "pretrain Zoobot self-supervised on real unlabeled survey images, then fine-tune for lens finding" -- since Zoobot itself is the fine-tuning backbone referenced -- making it the most directly applicable lead for a Zoobot-based lens pipeline, even though lens finding is not the task evaluated in the cited sources. [Inference, not a citation.]

### Gaps
- No source found reports a quantitative completeness/purity/efficiency number specifically for lens-finding using Stein et al.'s self-supervised DESI embeddings (the paper demonstrates the similarity-search application but the abstract-level content fetched did not include a lens-specific metric) -- the full paper or a follow-up application paper may have this; not retrievable within the tool budget used here.
- No source directly combines (a) self-supervised pretraining on unlabeled real survey images with (b) fine-tuning on a simulated lens-labeled dataset, which is the exact scenario posed in the research objective; the closest analogues split this into either real-unlabeled-pretrain-for-non-lens-tasks (Hayat, Stein, Walmsley) or sim-unlabeled-pretrain-for-lens-tasks (LenSiam) but not both halves together for lens finding.
- Could not confirm whether Zoobot's own codebase/papers (Walmsley et al.) have tested a self-supervised (as opposed to GZ-label-supervised) pretraining variant specifically fine-tuned for lens finding; the hybrid-pretraining paper (arXiv:2206.11927) evaluates general morphology tasks, not lens finding.

## Are there direct quantitative comparisons of "simulation-only training" vs. "simulation + domain-gap-closing technique" for an astronomical classification task, with before/after numbers?

### Takeaway
Yes, and these are the strongest findings of this research pass -- the domain-adaptation papers above collectively provide numerous explicit, paired before/after comparisons on astronomical classification/regression tasks (lens finding, lens-parameter regression, dark-matter-substructure classification, galaxy morphology, galaxy mergers), several of which are sim-to-real specifically; the GAN-based and self-supervised technique areas provide fewer but still concrete paired comparisons; and a large-scale non-technique-specific baseline (Euclid Q1) quantifies just how large the raw sim-to-real gap is before any of these techniques are applied.

### Cited Findings
- Euclid Quick Data Release Q1 strong-lens-finding work ("simulations to sky," referenced via search aggregation; likely part of the Euclid Q1 strong-lensing discovery-engine paper series -- exact arXiv ID not independently confirmed in this pass): a network trained only on simulations recovers up to 92% of simulated lenses at 100% purity, but only achieves 50% completeness at 24% purity when applied to real Euclid Q1 data (63 deg^2 sky area) -- a stark, explicit sim-only before-number. Augmenting training data with real Euclid lenses and non-lenses (outside this thread's technique scope, but the comparison point used in the source) improves completeness by 25-30% (~20 points from including real lenses, ~5-10 points from more diverse non-lenses/false positives), with the best strategy combining simulation diversity with real-lens fidelity. -- [Search-aggregated; likely Euclid Q1 strong-lensing series, e.g. companion to arXiv:2603.28580 "Bright and low-redshift strong lenses"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.28580)
- DeepMerge II (Ćiprijanović et al., arXiv:2103.01373): sim-to-real galaxy-merger classification accuracy without domain adaptation = 50%; with MMD + transfer learning domain adaptation = 69% -- a ~19-percentage-point before/after gain directly attributable to the domain-adaptation technique (see Q1 above for full detail). -- [arXiv:2103.01373](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2103.01373)

### Inferences
- Taken together, the magnitude of reported gains (roughly 9-41 percentage points of accuracy/F1/AUROC, or an "order of magnitude" in the GAN-pipeline case) across every technique area surveyed here indicates that domain-gap-closing techniques are not a marginal refinement but typically the dominant factor separating a sim-only lens/galaxy classifier from one that generalizes acceptably to real survey data -- consistent with the raw gap sizes reported in the sim-only Euclid Q1 baseline (92%/100% sim-recovery/purity collapsing to 50%/24% completeness/purity on real data). This suggests that for a sim-trained lens classifier, domain adaptation (best-evidenced across many papers) and/or self-supervised real-image pretraining (best architecturally matched to a Zoobot pipeline) are the most literature-supported next techniques to prioritize, ahead of domain randomization and GAN translation (both plausible but empirically thinner for this specific task).
